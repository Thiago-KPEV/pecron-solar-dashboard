Generation heatmap — when the sun actually reaches the panel

Live

The most informative chart here. Hours down, days across. If something shades the panel in the morning or afternoon, those edges show up as hard boundaries rather than a guess. Over months it also shows them drift as the sun's angle changes through the year — the difference between "something shades us" and knowing exactly what it costs, month by month.

Daily harvest divided by panel rating — the standard solar yardstick. It makes this array comparable to any other, and quantifies what shading or orientation costs. Set peakSunHoursNorm in site.config.js to compare against your region. It is the figure that settles an argument about relocating the panel.

Distinguishes "the sun ran the loads" from "the battery ran them and the sun refilled it later". A system can be net-positive over a day while running almost entirely off storage — this separates the two.

For the operator — internal

Operational. None of this is for outside eyes, and it's where the engineering value sits.

Overhead trend — the early warning

Live

Inverter idle and conversion loss, as a line rather than a daily figure. Its slope is the signal: a slow creep upward means something got left on or something is degrading, and a step down shows a change that worked. Probably keep this one private — "our solar system loses 300 Wh a day" is true, useful, and terrible marketing.

Pack life consumed

Live

Cumulative energy discharged divided by pack capacity — the honest measure of battery wear, far better than counting days. At the current rate you can project a replacement year rather than being surprised by one.

Pack health drift

Projected

Resting voltage at a fixed charge level, tracked over months. When the line sags, the pack is aging. Comparisons are only valid within a charge bucket — averaging across them would show a pack "improving" whenever a day happened to sit at a higher charge.

With extra metering hardware

The battery reports totals only. Attributing energy to a fan or a bike charger from those totals means watching numbers move right after something is switched on — that's inference, and it won't answer "what does a bike charge actually cost".

Illustrative, not measured. With seconds-resolution polling, loads become separable by their power signature; with cheap metering plugs they're measured outright. This turns one "delivered 430 Wh" figure into an itemised list — and gives a real number for a bike charge instead of an estimate.
